1 · Motivation

Beams carry loads by internal shear and bending

Beams are long, straight members loaded perpendicular to their axis.

Cut a beam at any point and the two pieces must still be in equilibrium. The material at the cut transmits an internal shear force V (perpendicular to the axis) and an internal bending moment M. With loads applied perpendicular to the axis, the normal force is zero and is not considered here.

For design we need V and M at every point, especially the largest values. Plotting them against the position x gives the shear diagram and the moment diagram.

Key idea. V(x) and M(x) usually change their formula wherever a concentrated force or couple acts, or a distributed load starts, stops or changes. So the beam is split into regions between these points, and each region has its own V(x) and M(x).

Memory aids: positive shear tends to rotate the segment clockwise; positive moment bends the beam concave upward (it "holds water"). When we cut a beam and keep the left segment, V is drawn down and M counterclockwise on its right face.

4 · Section 7.3 · Relations among distributed load, shear and moment

Equilibrium of a tiny beam element

Distributed load w(x) is taken positive upward. Step through the derivation.

dV/dx = w(x) (7–1)
Slope of the shear diagram = load intensity.
ΔV = ∫ w dx (7–2)
Change in shear = area under the load curve.
dM/dx = V (7–3)
Slope of the moment diagram = shear.
ΔM = ∫ V dx (7–4)
Change in moment = area under the shear diagram.
ΔV = F (7–5)
An upward force makes V jump up by F.
ΔM = M₀ (7–6)
A clockwise couple makes M jump up by M₀.

6 · Shape rules

Read the shapes from the load

If w is a polynomial of degree n, V has degree n + 1 and M has degree n + 2.

Load in a region Shear diagram V Moment diagram M
No load (w = 0) Constant (horizontal line) Linear (sloped straight line, slope = V)
Uniform load w = const Linear, slope = w Parabolic
Linearly varying load Parabolic Cubic
Concentrated force F Jump of F (up for an upward force) Kink (sudden change of slope)
Concentrated couple M₀ No effect Jump of M₀ (up for a clockwise couple)
V = 0 at a point — M has a local maximum or minimum there

8 · Procedures for analysis

Two ways to draw the diagrams

Method of sections (Section 7.2)

  1. Support reactions. Find all reactions from the free-body diagram of the whole beam.
  2. Regions. Measure x from the left end. Start a new region at every concentrated force or couple and wherever a distributed load starts, stops or changes.
  3. Section. In each region, cut at a general x and draw the left segment with V and M in their positive sense.
  4. Equations. ΣFy = 0 gives V(x); ΣM about the cut gives M(x).
  5. Plot. Positive values above the axis, negative below.

Graphical method (Section 7.3)

  1. Support reactions as before.
  2. Shear diagram. Start at x = 0 with the end value. Slope = w; jump by each concentrated force; ΔV = area under w.
  3. Moment diagram. Start at the end value. Slope = V; ΔM = area under V; jump by each couple (up for clockwise).
  4. Maximum moment. Where V = 0 (or where V jumps through zero), find |M|max.
  5. Check. The diagrams must close: V and M at the right end equal the end conditions.
