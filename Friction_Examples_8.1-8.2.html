Remember

Procedure (Section 8.2)

  1. Free-body diagram: unless the problem says motion impends or slipping occurs, show every friction force as an unknown (do not assume F = µN).
  2. Count unknowns and equilibrium equations. More unknowns → apply F = µN at some or all contacts (or a tipping condition).
  3. Sense of F: an unknown equilibrium friction force may be drawn either way (a negative answer reverses it). When you use F = µN, draw F opposing the impending relative motion.
  4. Type 1 (no apparent impending motion): solve equilibrium, then check F ≤ µsN at every contact (and that N lies under the body).
  5. Type 2 (impending at all contacts): use F = µsN everywhere.
  6. Type 3 (impending at some contacts): assume each possible slip or tip, solve, check the other contacts, and keep the case that needs the smallest load.
  7. Sliding: F = µkN (kinetic), acting opposite to the motion.
