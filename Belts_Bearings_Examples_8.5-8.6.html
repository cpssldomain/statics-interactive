Belt and Bearing Friction — Worked Examples

Ten problems: Examples 8.8 and 8.9 and eight extra problems (8–86, 8–87, 8–94, 8–95, 8–103, 8–107, 8–110, 8–114). Find the wrap angle, the tensions, or the friction moment of a bearing, and check each step.

Remember

Procedure (Sections 8.5–8.6)

  1. Belts and ropes: identify the direction of impending (or actual) slip of the belt relative to the surface. T2 pulls in that direction, T1 resists it; T2 = T1eµβ, T2 > T1.
  2. β in radians: n full turns add 2πn. The radius of the drum does not matter.
  3. Free pulleys (turning on good bearings) do not change the tension; only fixed drums and pegs do.
  4. Bearings with uniform pressure: collar M = (2/3)µP(R2³ − R1³)/(R2² − R1²); pivot M = (2/3)µPR. Check whether the figure gives radii or diameters.
  5. Non-uniform pressure: write p (or w) as a function of position, find its peak from ΣF = 0, then integrate dM = r µ dN.
  6. Constant speed: use µk; impending motion: µs.
