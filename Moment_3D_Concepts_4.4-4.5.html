1 · Section 4.4 · Eqs. 4–6 to 4–8

The moment of a force about a point: MO = r × F

r runs from O to any point on the line of action of F. The magnitude is MO = rF sin θ = Fd, and the direction follows the right-hand rule: curl the fingers from r toward F and the thumb gives MO, perpendicular to the plane of r and F.

Drag to rotate. The shaded triangle is the plane of r and F; MO is perpendicular to it.
Check against the book. Ex. 4.5: MO = {−16.5i + 5.51j} kN·m. Ex. 4.4: MO = {−98.6k} N·m. For 2-D problems the book prefers the scalar method (M = Fd); the vector method pays off in 3-D, where the arm d is hard to find.
2 · Principle of transmissibility (Fig. 4–13)

Any point on the line of action gives the same moment

MO = r1 × F = r2 × F = r3 × F. A force can slide along its line of action without changing its moment about O: it is a sliding vector. Move the point of application with the slider and watch r change while MO stays fixed.

Uses the force from Section 1 (Ex. 4.5 by default). Points A and B of the book are marked.
At the closest point r is perpendicular to F, so |r| equals the moment arm d and MO = Fd directly. Everywhere else |r| > d, but sin θ shrinks to compensate.
3 · Physical meaning of Eq. 4–8 (Fig. 4–14b)

Each term is one force component times one coordinate

(MO)x = ryFz − rzFy: Fx is parallel to the x axis and gives nothing; Fz acts at distance ry from the x axis; Fy acts at height rz. Pick a component to see its own moment about O.

Contributions to MO (same r and F as Section 1). A blank cell is zero because the component is parallel to that axis.

5 · Section 4.5 · Eqs. 4–10 to 4–12

Moment about a specified axis: Ma = ua · (r × F)

A nut or a shaft can only turn about one axis. Only the projection of MO onto that axis matters. Take any point O on the axis, compute MO = r × F, then project with the unit vector ua. Scalar form: Ma = F da, with da the shortest distance between the line of action and the axis.

Grey dash-dot line: the axis a. Faint purple: MO about the chosen axis point. Solid purple on the axis: Ma = (ua·MO) ua.

7 · Procedure and important points

How to solve these problems

Moment about a point (4.4)

  1. Set up x, y, z axes. Write F as a Cartesian vector, usually F u with u from two points on its line.
  2. Write r from O to any convenient point on the line of action (choose one that makes zeros).
  3. Evaluate the determinant |i j k; rx ry rz; Fx Fy Fz|. Keep the order r × F.
  4. For several forces, add the r × F vectors (Eq. 4–9). Direction angles come from the unit vector of the result.

Moment about an axis (4.5)

  1. Find ua from two points on the axis.
  2. Pick r from any point on the axis to any point on the line of action of F.
  3. Ma = |uax uay uaz; rx ry rz; Fx Fy Fz|.
  4. A positive Ma acts along +ua; negative, opposite. As a vector: Ma = Maua.
  5. No moment if F is parallel to the axis or its line of action passes through the axis.
