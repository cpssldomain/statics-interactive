Dry Friction — Concepts

Friction is a tangential force that resists sliding. It grows with the push until it reaches Fs = µsN, then drops to Fk = µkN once sliding starts. Move the sliders to see when a body holds, slips or tips, and how to tell which kind of friction problem you have.

Pull a block: static friction, impending motion, sliding — or tipping

A block of weight W is pulled by a horizontal force P at height h. While it holds, F = P and the normal force N shifts a distance x = Ph/W to balance the tipping effect of P. Friction can grow only up to Fs = µsN; after that the block slides and F = µkN. If N reaches the corner first (x = b/2), the block tips instead.

2 · Section 8.1 · Angle of static friction · Example 8.2

The angle of friction and the angle of repose

The resultant R of N and F makes the angle φ with the normal. At impending slip φ = φs = tan⁻¹µs. A block on an incline of angle θ is held while θ < φs; at θ = φs (the angle of repose) it is about to slide — whatever its weight. A tall block may tip first: G above the downhill corner when tan θ = b/H.

Purple: the angle φ between R and the normal; dashed: the limit φs. The vertical through G (teal dashed) must stay inside the base, or the block tips.

7 · Section 8.2

Procedure for analysis

  1. Free-body diagrams. Unless the problem says motion impends or slipping occurs, show friction forces as unknowns (do not assume F = µN).
  2. Count the unknowns and compare with the equilibrium equations. If there are more unknowns, apply F = µN at some or all contacts (or a tipping condition).
  3. Sense. If you use F = µN, draw F with its correct sense (opposing the impending relative motion).
  4. Solve the equilibrium and friction equations; for 3-D systems use Cartesian vectors.
  5. Check: every F ≤ µsN where slipping was not assumed, and N inside the contact area (no tipping). If a check fails, try the other case.
Book note: the third "Important Point" on p. 394 prints Fs = µsN for the sliding case; for sliding it should read Fk = µkN (Eq. 8–2).
